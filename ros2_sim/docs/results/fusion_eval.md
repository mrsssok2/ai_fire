# Fusion evaluation: flame sensors vs CNN vs fused

Generated by `scripts/eval_fusion.py`; raw per-run data in `fusion_eval.json`. 6 seeds per scenario, a fresh Gazebo
run each (real simulated camera + my CNN + simulated flame sensors with 10% signal noise).

**Protocol.** The robot starts at the origin and turns once on the spot (360 deg at 0.6 rad/s, about 10 s) with fires and decoys
placed around it at 0.6-1.3 m (seeded). Three alarm rules run on the same sensor and camera data:
*flame sensors alone* (any sensor's digital output trips), *CNN alone* (confidence >= 0.5 on the latest frame, whole frame or an
HSV-proposed box), *fused* (flame sensors tripped AND weighted score 0.4 * p_flame + 0.6 * p_cnn >= 0.6, CNN reading held 1 s; weights
fixed beforehand, not tuned on these scenes; see `core/fusion.py`). Decoys: red box / barrel and orange cone (look like fire, no IR),
sunlit window (bright, IR 0.8 of an intensity-1 fire), hot lamp (bright orange bulb, IR 1.6). A flame-sensor range of ~1 m for an intensity-1 fire is my
assumption, so fires placed farther than that are out of the sensors' reach by construction.

**Counting.** Alarm samples are grouped by what caused them (ground truth) into events. An event on a fire is a detection; an event on a
decoy or on nothing is a **false alarm** (counted once per decoy that raised an alarm in a run, plus once per event on nothing); a fire with no event during the full turn is **missed**. Time to detect is from the start of the
turn to the first detection of that fire. Aim error: |flame-sensor bearing - true bearing| over the detection samples (the bearing is a weighted mean
of the three sensor axes, so it is coarse).

Seeds only change positions, fire sizes and noise, not the CNN, so the numbers are a small sample, not a benchmark.

## What the numbers say

* **Fusion removes the decoys that only fool the camera, not the ones that fool both.** Red box / red barrel / orange cone: the CNN alone
  raised an alarm on 14 of 18, the fused rule on 0 (they carry no IR, so the flame sensors never trip). The **hot lamp** (12 / 12) and
  the **sunlit window** (7 / 12 fused, 8 / 12 flame sensors) trip the flame sensors *and* the CNN rates them as fire (CNN: 12 / 12 for both),
  so fusion passes them. In `decoys only` every run still has false alarms for flame sensors and fused (11 of 24 decoys each); the CNN alone
  has 21 of 24.
* **Fused cannot see farther than the flame sensors.** All 15 fires within 1.13 m were found by every method; of the 9 fires at 1.14-1.3 m
  the flame sensors found 2 and the CNN all 9. So over the 24 fires the flame sensors miss 7 and fused 8 (range assumed 1 m for an intensity-1
  fire), the CNN alone misses none. One fire the flame sensors found (1.14 m) was not confirmed by the CNN, which is the only extra miss of
  fused over flame-only.
* **The CNN alone detects everything and flags nearly everything**: no misses, but 39 false alarms in 18 runs (flame sensors 20, fused 19). The README finding about
  the CNN (27 of 35 hard non-fire photos flagged) shows up here too; the decoy looks are my own models, a real camera would differ.
* **Time to detect** (from the start of the 360 deg turn; depends on where the objects are): 3-5 s for all three; fused is a little later than
  flame-only (4.9 vs 4.1 s, 3.2 vs 3.0 s) because the fixed camera has to turn to the fire before the CNN confirms.
* **Aim:** the flame-sensor bearing is within 5-14 deg of the true direction on average (coarse: weighted mean of three sensor axes).
* Sample size is small (6 seeds x 3 scenarios, same CNN throughout); read the table as "what happens in this simulator", not as a benchmark.
  A fusion that only helps when the CNN is better than the user's current one would need a better CNN or an extra cue (flicker, shape).
